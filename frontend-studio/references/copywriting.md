# Copywriting in design

Words appear in an interface for one reason: to make it easier to understand and use. They are design material, not decoration, and generic copy makes a design feel as templated as generic layout does. Bring the same restraint to words that you bring to spacing and color.

## Before writing

Ask what this screen needs to say and the shortest clear way to say it. If the brief contains real content, use it. If not, write realistic content for the specific subject and audience, never lorem ipsum.

## Voice

- **Write from the user's side.** Name things by what users understand, not how the system is built: "Notifications", not "Webhook config".
- **Describe, do not sell.** Say plainly what something is or does. Specific and legible beats clever.
- **Active voice, plain verbs, sentence case, no filler.** Match the tone to brand and audience, from playful to strictly professional.
- **One job per element.** A heading orients, a description explains, a button acts. Do not make one element do all three.
- **Write in the user's language and locale,** including number, date and currency formats.

## Actions

- **A button says exactly what happens:** "Save changes", "Send invoice", "Book session", not "Submit" or "OK".
- **Keep the verb through the whole flow:** the "Publish" button produces a "Published" confirmation, not "Success!".
- **Consistent vocabulary is navigation.** If it is a "project" on one screen, it is not a "workspace" on another.
- **Destructive actions name the object and consequence:** "Delete 3 photos permanently".

## Failure and emptiness

- **Errors explain what happened and how to fix it,** in the interface's voice. They do not apologize and are never vague: "Card declined. Check the number or try another card", not "Oops, something went wrong".
- **Empty states invite action:** say what will appear here and put the first action right there.
- **Loading text, if any, says what is happening,** not jokes by default.

## Realistic placeholder content

- **Names:** varied and fitting the audience and locale. Avoid the famous placeholder names (John Doe, Jane Smith, Jan Kowalski).
- **Brands:** invent names that fit the industry and tone. Avoid Acme, Nexus, Quantum and similar.
- **Numbers:** organic, not round (47.2%, 1,284 orders, 3 min 12 s), consistent with each other across the screen.
- **Dates and times:** plausible and relative where it helps ("2 hours ago", "Due Friday").
- **Avoid clichés:** elevate, seamless, next-gen, unleash, revolutionize, supercharge, empower, cutting-edge.
- **Avoid redundant labels:** "12 left" instead of "Stock: 12", when the context already explains the value.

## Micro-checks before delivery

- Can any sentence lose half its words without losing meaning?
- Does every button start with a verb that names the result?
- Are error messages specific and fixable?
- Would a first-time user understand every label without training?
